Every Street Fighter has a resource that decides more matches than raw damage. In SF6 it is the Drive Gauge: the six green bars under your health. Almost every system tool runs on it, from your parry to your enhanced specials, and running out of it puts you in Burnout, a state so dangerous that it often decides the round by itself. A useful way to think about it is as a second health bar: your opponent can't KO you by draining it, but once it's empty, they can KO you much more easily.

If you only remember one thing from this chapter: the player with more Drive Gauge usually has more options. Spending it is fine when it buys damage, a knockdown or your turn. Spending it out of panic is how players walk into Burnout.

The Gauge: How It Fills and Empties

Each round starts with all 6 bars full. Unlike the Super Art gauge (3 bars, which carries over between rounds), the Drive Gauge resets every round.

What refills it

  • Time. It refills on its own. Walking forward refills it noticeably faster (roughly 50% faster than standing still); walking back or standing refill it at about the base rate; being in the air slows it down.
  • Your attacks. Your moves build gauge whether they hit or are blocked.
  • Successful parries. Parrying a strike refunds 1 bar, a projectile 0.5, and a Super Art or Drive Impact 2 bars.
  • Throw techs. Escaping a throw gives you half a bar.

What drains it

  • Spending it on Drive tools and OD moves.
  • Blocking. Blocked attacks, especially specials and Super Arts, drain your gauge instead of your health. That's why SF6 has no chip damage while you still have gauge.
  • Punish Counters and Drive Impacts that hit you take gauge away.
  • Holding Drive Parry keeps draining it for as long as you hold.

Regeneration also pauses briefly after you spend gauge and while you're blocking, so a player who spends and then gets put on defense can sink surprisingly fast. One more detail: if you Drive Rush in the middle of a combo, you stop building gauge for the rest of that combo.

Drive Impact: The Armored Question

How it works

Drive Impact has 26 frames of startup, which is slow, but it has armor that absorbs two hits. If your opponent presses a normal button into it, the Drive Impact eats the hit and connects anyway. You still take half of the absorbed damage as recoverable (grey) health.

  • On hit: roughly 800 damage, a knockdown, and the opponent loses a bar of Drive Gauge.
  • On block in the middle of the screen: it pushes them back and you're only −3, so it's relatively safe.
  • Near the wall: it causes a wall splat, on hit and on block, which gives you a free follow-up combo.
  • Against a Burnout opponent near the wall: even if they block it, they are stunned for a free combo. This is the main way Burnout turns into a lost round.
  • As a Punish Counter or after absorbing a hit: the opponent crumples, which also gives you a full combo.

Combos that start from Drive Impact take a 20% damage penalty, so its value comes more from the knockdown, the wall splat and the gauge it drains than from raw damage.

How to beat it

  • Your own Drive Impact. The best reaction answer: it armors through theirs and the later Drive Impact wins as a Punish Counter. Practice this in training mode until it is automatic.
  • Throw it if you are close enough.
  • Jump over it if you see it early.
  • Moves with 3 or more hits, or armor-breaking moves: most Super Arts, Drive Reversal, and some OD moves beat the armor.
  • Parry it. A successful parry refunds 2 bars.

Most beginners lose to Drive Impact because they keep pressing buttons into it. The fix is not a technique but a habit: when you see the dark flash and the big wind-up, answer with your own Drive Impact.

Don't throw Drive Impact out from far away for no reason. Experienced players react to it with their own Drive Impact every time. It works best when the opponent is pressing buttons they can't cancel, when they're cornered, and above all when they're in Burnout.

Drive Parry and Perfect Parry

Drive Parry

Holding MP+MK puts you in a parry stance that stops every strike and projectile, high or low, from its first frame. The parry window is 12 frames and continues while you hold it. A successful parry puts you in about the same situation as blocking that move, but instead of losing gauge, you get gauge back: 1 bar for a strike, 0.5 for a projectile, 2 for a Super Art or Drive Impact.

The catch: parry loses to throws. If you're thrown while parrying (or during the recovery of a parry that didn't catch anything), it's a Punish Counter throw: 70% extra damage, a hard knockdown you can't back rise from, it can't be teched, and you lose another Drive bar. Opponents who see you parrying a lot will simply walk up and throw you. Parrying a predictable fireball from a distance is safe; parrying at point-blank range is a guess.

Perfect Parry

If your parry catches an attack in its first 2 frames, and, against strikes, you're also holding the correct block direction (down-back against lows, back against mids and overheads), you get a Perfect Parry. The screen freezes, the opponent can't cancel their move, and you recover almost instantly, which usually means a guaranteed punish.

Two limits keep it from being overwhelming:

  • A punish after a Perfect Parry deals only 50% damage.
  • Against projectiles, a Perfect Parry has a fixed recovery, so it usually doesn't give you a punish from far away.

Perfect Parry works best as a read: you expect a specific attack (a fireball, a predictable poke, a jump-in) and parry it right as it arrives. Don't build your defense around it.

When parrying pays off, and when it doesn't

These rules of thumb are adapted from gootecks' advice on parry-type moves and checked against how Drive Parry works in SF6:

  • Projectiles from mid-screen or further: good. You see them coming, the parry refunds the half bar it cost, and you can Drive Rush forward out of it.
  • Jump-ins: a normal parry leaves you about where blocking would. It's worth it when you're going for a Perfect Parry or want the gauge back.
  • Light attacks: little reward. You rarely get a punish, and the opponent can mix in a throw.
  • On wakeup at close range: risky. A meaty throw beats it and becomes a Punish Counter throw.
  • Between the hits of a block string: only when you know there's a gap and what comes next; a delayed hit or a throw catches you.

Tip: you can assign Drive Parry (MP+MK) to a single button in the control settings, which makes it easier to press on time.

Drive Rush: The Engine of SF6 Offense

Two ways to Drive Rush

  • From Parry (1 bar total): press MP+MK, then quickly double-tap forward. A fast, relatively cheap way to cover ground in neutral.
  • Canceled from a normal (3 bars): cancel a cancelable normal (for example, 2MK) into Drive Rush. This is how players extend combos and keep pressure after a blocked attack.

The key benefit: the first normal you do out of a Drive Rush gets +4 frames on hit and on block. A move that is normally −2 on block becomes +2, so you keep your turn. A move that normally doesn't combo might combo after a Drive Rush.

The costs you don't see

  • Gauge: two Drive Rush cancels are a full 6 bars. Players who cancel into Drive Rush every time they're blocked are often in Burnout by the middle of the round.
  • Counter-hit state: the whole dash counts as a counter-hit state. If you get hit or thrown during it, you take extra damage, and a throw during Drive Rush can't be teched.
  • Combo scaling: Drive Rushing in the middle of a combo applies an extra 15% damage penalty to the rest of the combo (once per combo), and you stop building gauge for the rest of that combo.

Drive Reversal and OD Moves

Drive Reversal (2 bars)

Press forward + HP+HK while you're in blockstun (or right after a successful parry), and your character does an invincible counterattack that knocks the opponent away. Since 2024, you can also do it on wakeup. It is the standard way to escape pressure for characters without an invincible uppercut, or when you can't afford a guess.

  • It deals only 500 damage, all of it recoverable (grey) health, so it can't KO.
  • It is −6 on block, so a blocked or baited Drive Reversal can be punished.
  • You can't use it in Burnout, which is exactly when you'd want it most.

Think of it as "buying your way out" of pressure: it costs a third of your gauge and gets you back to neutral, not a big reward.

OD moves (2 bars)

Doing a special move with two buttons of the same type (for example 236 + LP+MP) gives you its Overdrive version, which is what earlier games called an EX move. OD versions usually get extra hits, invincibility, more damage or better frame data. An OD uppercut is often a character's most reliable invincible reversal; an OD fireball often beats your opponent's normal fireball.

Because each OD move costs 2 bars, three of them empty your gauge. Use them when they win something specific: a reversal you need, a combo extension that leads to a knockdown, a fireball war you need to win.

Burnout: Why an Empty Gauge Loses Rounds

When your Drive Gauge hits zero, you enter Burnout. Until the gauge refills, you are playing a much weaker version of your character.

In Burnout What it means in practice
No Drive tools No Drive Impact, Parry, Rush, Reversal or OD moves. Your defense is down to blocking, your normal reversals and Super Arts.
+4 frames of blockstun on everything you block Every attacker's move is 4 frames better against you. Moves that were safe to press against become frame traps, and your opponent keeps their turn.
Chip damage Blocked specials and Super Arts now take 25% of their damage from your health, and that chip can KO you. Normals still don't chip.
Stun from Drive Impact near the wall A Drive Impact that pushes you into the wall stuns you even if you block it, giving your opponent a free combo.

Getting out

Burnout ends when the gauge refills on its own, which takes somewhere around 15 to 20 seconds. Walking forward, landing hits and blocking all speed it up. Whatever you do, get out of the corner: Burnout in the middle of the screen is uncomfortable; Burnout in the corner is where Drive Impact stuns happen.

When your opponent is in Burnout

  • Push them to the corner and look for a Drive Impact that wall splats into a stun.
  • Pressure with specials: every blocked special now chips their health.
  • Use your +4 blockstun advantage to keep pressure going with buttons that are normally unsafe.
  • Don't let them breathe: the longer the round goes, the closer they are to a full gauge again.

Managing the Gauge: A Simple Budget

Top players don't follow a fixed rule, but beginners improve quickly with a simple budget:

Your gauge How to play
5–6 bars Spend freely on things that win: Drive Rush combos, OD moves in combos, Drive Rush pressure.
3–4 bars Spend on clear rewards: a combo that ends in a knockdown, an OD reversal you need. Avoid 3-bar Drive Rush cancels on block.
1–2 bars Danger zone. Keep 2 bars for one Drive Reversal or OD reversal if you're being pressured. Walk forward and land pokes to rebuild.
Burnout Leave the corner, block carefully, look for your own reversals and Super Arts, and avoid blocking specials if you can jump or walk away.

Common Drive mistakes

  • Pressing buttons into Drive Impact instead of answering it with your own.
  • Canceling into Drive Rush after every blocked poke. Two cancels and your gauge is gone.
  • Holding parry at close range. Your opponent walks up and throws you for 70% extra damage.
  • Using Drive Reversal at low gauge. It saves you once and puts you one step from Burnout.
  • Ignoring your opponent's gauge. Watch theirs as closely as yours: a player at 1 bar can't afford to parry or Drive Reverse, and a player in Burnout is your best chance at a big lead.

Lab drill: answering Drive Impact (10 minutes)

  1. In training mode, record the dummy doing a Drive Impact from mid range, with a few seconds of walking before it.
  2. Set the recording to play back at random intervals mixed with plain walking, so you can't predict it.
  3. Every time you see the flash, answer with your own Drive Impact (HP+HK). Count successes out of 20.
  4. Once you're above 15 out of 20, switch the dummy to do Drive Impact right after one of your blocked pokes, the situation where it usually catches people.
