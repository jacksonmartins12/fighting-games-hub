Weekly Progress Tracker Template

UniversalThe ideas in this chapter apply to any fighting game. Examples come from SF6, and sections marked SF6 only apply to it. For how other games differ, see Fighting Game Families.

You can't improve what you don't measure. This template is designed for you to print out weekly and track specific metrics. Seeing progression week-to-week is incredibly motivating and helps you identify what's actually working.

How to Use This Tracker

Weekly Ritual (Sunday Evening)

Step 1: Print or write down a blank copy of this template.

Step 2: Answer each question honestly (don't exaggerate).

Step 3: Compare to last week. Did specific metrics improve?

Step 4: Next week, focus your training on areas that didn't improve.

Tracking Over Months: The Bigger Picture

Monthly Review (End of Month)

After 4 weeks of weekly tracking, do this:

  • Compare Week 1 vs Week 4 metrics: Did execution improve? Footsies? Anti-air? Adaptation? Mental game? Rank?
  • Identify trending improvements: Week 1: 60% combos. Week 2: 65%. Week 3: 70%. Week 4: 75%. Clear improvement.
  • Identify stalled metrics: Week 1-4: Always 50% anti-air success. Needs focus next month.
  • Psychological growth: Week 1: Tilted after 2 losses. Week 4: Tilted after 5 losses. Mental resilience improved.

3-Month Milestone Review

Every 3 months, take stock:

  • Rank progression: Started at Rank X. Now at Rank Y. Progression = real.
  • Tournament results: Month 1: 0-2 record. Month 3: 2-3 record. Improvement visible.
  • Skill mastery: Identify which skills are now "automatic" (execute without thinking) vs. still conscious effort.
  • Matchup knowledge: Month 1: Lost all unfamiliar matchups. Month 3: Lose 50% of unfamiliar matchups. Knowledge accumulated.

Expected Plateaus

Every 1-2 months, you'll hit a plateau. Metrics don't improve for 2-3 weeks. This is normal. Often your skills are still consolidating even when the numbers stand still.

When plateau happens:

  • Don't panic. Don't increase volume. Add variety instead.
  • Try new strategies. Lab different setups. Play different opponents.
  • Rest more (sleep + mental breaks). Plateaus often resolve with rest.
  • After a few weeks of plateau, growth usually resumes. This is the common pattern.
