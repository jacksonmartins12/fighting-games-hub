Quick Matchup Reference Chart

SF6 moduleChecked against Street Fighter 6 in October 2026. Balance patches can change numbers and systems; when they do, the fundamentals in The Path still apply.

Once you're intermediate, understanding character matchups accelerates improvement. This chart uses the Street Fighter 6 roster as its example, grouped by archetype. Archetypes are a starting map, not a rulebook: most SF6 characters blend styles, and patches shift strengths over time.

How Matchups Are Rated

The community rates matchups as a ratio of expected wins out of 10 games between players of equal skill.

5-5 (Even): Neither character has a structural edge. Execution, reads and neutral decide it.

6-4 (Slight advantage): One character's tools line up well against the other's. Noticeable, but very winnable for the underdog.

7-3 or worse (Strong advantage): The disadvantaged side must play very precisely and take calculated risks. Rare in modern, well-balanced games.

Treat published matchup charts as opinions from top players, not facts. At beginner and intermediate level, player skill matters far more than the matchup number.

Shoto / All-Rounder Ryu, Ken, Akuma, Terry A projectile, an invincible uppercut, solid normals and good damage. Can play every range reasonably well. Respect the uppercut on wakeup and when you jump. Learn which of their fireballs you can jump or Drive Parry on reaction. Win by out-spacing their key normal rather than trying to out-tool them. No single overwhelming strength. Uppercut is very punishable if blocked or baited.
Rushdown Cammy, Jamie, Kimberly, Blanka, Ed, Rashid, C. Viper, Yasmine Fast movement and strong close-range offense. Wants to get in, stay in, and force guesses. Stay calm under pressure: block, look for gaps in their strings, and use your fastest normal or a Drive Reversal when you find one. Anti-air consistently, because many rushdown characters rely on jumping or fast approaches. Shorter-range normals. Many approaches are unsafe or committal when read. Lower defensive tools when they are cornered.
Zoner Guile, Dhalsim, JP, A.K.I., Mai, Sagat Long-range normals and projectiles. Wants to keep you at a distance and wear you down. Advance in small steps rather than one big jump. Learn which projectiles you can Drive Parry, jump on reaction, or beat with Drive Impact. Once in, keep them in the corner; zoners usually struggle there. Weaker when you are close. Projectiles and long limbs have recovery you can whiff-punish.
Grappler Zangief, Manon, Lily, Alex Command grabs that beat blocking. Each close-range touch can lead to big damage and a strong knockdown. Stay outside their command-grab range and poke them as they walk in. Jump or backdash when you expect a grab, and press fast buttons when you expect them to walk up. Don't block passively at point-blank range. Slower movement and fewer long-range tools. Whiffed command grabs are punishable.
Mid-Range / Footsies Chun-Li, Dee Jay, Juri, Luke, Marisa, Elena, M. Bison, Arjun Excellent normals at medium distance. Win by controlling the space just outside your reach. Don't fight their best normal head-on. Whiff-punish it or stay just outside its range. Look for openings at ranges where their buttons are weaker, often very close or very far. Can be overwhelmed by strong close-range pressure or out-ranged by zoners.
Defensive / Special E. Honda, Ingrid Unusual tools that don't fit cleanly into one box (Honda's charge moves and headbutts, Ingrid's mobility). Lab their signature moves specifically: find which are punishable on block and how to beat their main approach. Specific to each character. Study individually.

Matchup-Specific Training Strategy

Step-by-Step Matchup Mastery

  1. Learn Their Best Tools: Watch 1-2 pro VODs. What do they use most? c.MK? Projectile? Command grab? Note it.
  2. Lab Your Answer: Spend 30 min in training mode. What move counters theirs? Write it down.
  3. Practice the Scenario: Set CPU to use that tool. Practice your counter 50x. Goal: automate response.
  4. Play vs Real Players: 10 matches vs that character. See how your lab answers work against real adaptation.
  5. Iterate: Find gaps. Adjust strategy. Repeat step 2-4.
