Fighting Game Glossary: 154 Essential Terms

Fighting games have their own language. Here's a glossary of terms you'll encounter in guides, Discord servers and tournament commentary. Terms marked (SF6) are specific to Street Fighter 6; the rest apply broadly. Under most terms you'll also find the Japanese name, useful when you watch Japanese players and content. The term list and the Japanese names draw on Infil's Fighting Game Glossary (glossary.infil.net), which has over 800 terms with video examples; the definitions here are written for SF6.

Core Concepts

Attack-Block-Throw Triangle:
The basic rock-paper-scissors of close range: attacks beat throws, throws beat blocking, and blocking beats attacks. Most close-range decisions come back to this triangle. 三すくみ (san sukumi)
Neutral:
The state where neither player has an advantage and both are maneuvering to land the first hit. Footsies happen in neutral. ニュートラル (nyūtoraru)
Footsies:
The mid-range game of spacing, pokes and whiff punishes that decides who wins neutral. The foundation of Street Fighter-style games. Players mix quick light and medium pokes to throw off the opponent's sense of distance, then whiff punish with a medium or heavy. 地上戦 (chijousen)
Spacing:
Controlling the distance between you and your opponent, ideally staying where your attacks reach them but theirs don't reach you.
Poke:
A quick, relatively safe attack, usually a normal with good range, used to control space or check the opponent. Not every normal is a poke. 牽制 (kensei)
Normal / Special / Super:
Normals are single-button attacks. Specials need a motion or charge input (fireball, uppercut). Supers (Super Arts in SF6) cost super meter and do high damage. 通常技 / 必殺技 (tsūjou waza / hissatsu waza)
Whiff:
An attack that misses completely. 空振り (karaburi)
Whiff Punish:
Hitting an opponent during the recovery of an attack they missed. A core footsies skill. 差し返し (sashikaeshi)
Punish:
Hitting an opponent during the recovery of a move that is unsafe on block or that whiffed. A "punish combo" is the best damage you can get from it. 確定反撃 (kakutei hangeki)
Anti-Air:
Hitting an opponent out of a jump, usually with an uppercut-style special or a normal that hits upward. 対空 (taikū)
Pressure / Offense:
Keeping the opponent blocking and guessing while you hold the initiative. プレッシャー (puresshā)
Mixup:
A situation where the defender has to guess between options that need different defenses (high/low, left/right, strike/throw). A wrong guess means getting hit; a correct guess means avoiding it. 択 (taku)
Crossup:
An attack, often a jump-in, that hits the opponent from behind so they have to block in the opposite direction from what they expect. めくり (mekuri)
Okizeme (Oki):
Offense applied to an opponent as they wake up from a knockdown. A good knockdown lets the attacker set up a mixup or a timed attack before the defender can act. 起き攻め (okizeme)
Wakeup:
The moment a knocked-down character stands up, and the options they have at that moment (block, reversal, backdash, etc.). 起き上がり (okiagari)
Reversal:
A move done on the first possible frame after blockstun, hitstun or wakeup, usually an invincible one, used to escape pressure. リバーサル (ribāsaru)
Bait:
Doing something that tempts the opponent into a predictable response, then punishing it. For example, faking an approach so they throw out an uppercut that you block and punish. 狩る (karu)
Read:
Predicting what the opponent will do based on their habits, then choosing the option that beats it. 読み (yomi)
Conditioning:
Repeating something until the opponent adapts to it, then switching to the option that beats their adaptation.
Adaptation:
Changing your approach during a match or set based on what the opponent is doing.
Shoto:
A character built like Ryu and Ken: a fireball, an uppercut-style anti-air and solid all-round normals. The classic archetype for learning fundamentals. 道着 (dougi)
Zoning / Zoner:
Controlling the screen from long range with projectiles and long normals to keep the opponent out. A zoner is a character built around it. 遠距離戦 (enkyori sen)
Rushdown:
A playstyle (and archetype) built around getting close and keeping the opponent under constant pressure. ガン攻め (gan seme)
Grappler:
A character built around command grabs, who wins by getting close and making blocking dangerous. 投げキャラ (nage kyara)
Turtling:
Playing very defensively, blocking and waiting for the opponent to make a mistake. In SF6, the Drive Gauge you lose while blocking limits how long it works. 待ち (machi)
Mashing:
Pressing buttons repeatedly or without a plan, often on wakeup or under pressure. Frame traps and meaties are built to punish it. 連打 (renda)
Lab / Labbing:
Studying a specific situation in training mode: recording the dummy, checking frame data, drilling a punish or a setup. 練習 (renshū)
50/50:
A mixup between two options where the defender has to guess, like a low or an overhead, or a crossup or not. Guess wrong and you get hit. Know which ones you can react to in SF6 (most overheads) and which you really have to guess. 2択 (ni taku)
Abare:
Japanese term for pressing a fast button during the opponent's pressure to interrupt it, usually in a gap. Good abare beats strings with gaps; it loses to frame traps, which exist to punish it. 暴れ (abare)
Check:
A quick poke or anti-air used to stop the opponent from moving forward or pressing buttons freely. Checking their walk forward makes them respect your range.
Turn:
Whose turn it is to attack. After a blocked move that leaves you plus, it's your turn; after a minus move, it's theirs. A lot of SF6 is about taking your turn back at the right moment and not pressing when it isn't yours. ターン (tān)
Respect:
Choosing to block or hold back because the opponent has shown you a dangerous option. Respecting a plus move means not pressing after it. A player who never respects anything gets frame trapped; one who respects everything gets thrown.
Open Up:
Getting through the opponent's defense with a mixup, a throw or a frame trap so that your attack finally lands. 崩し (kuzushi)
Yomi:
Japanese for "reading". The layers of prediction in a fight: knowing what they'll do, what they think you'll do, and so on. David Sirlin popularized the term in English. 読み (yomi)
Win Condition:
What a character or player needs to make happen to win, like a grappler getting in close or a zoner keeping the opponent far away. Knowing your win condition, and your opponent's, tells you what to focus on in a matchup.
Autopilot:
Doing the same thing in the same situation without thinking, like always pressing the same button after a knockdown. Opponents punish autopilot quickly; reviewing replays is how you find yours.
Flowchart:
A player whose decisions follow a fixed, predictable sequence ("if blocked, this; if it hits, that"). Calling someone a flowchart player means their patterns are easy to read.
Download:
Learning an opponent's habits during a set so you can predict and counter them. "He downloaded me" means the opponent figured out your patterns.
Knowledge Check:
A tactic that's easy to beat once you know the answer but wins against players who haven't seen it, like a specific setup or an unusual move. The fix is learning the answer in training mode, once.

Frame Data and Timing

Frame:
The smallest unit of time in the game. Most fighting games run at 60 frames per second, so 1 frame is about 16.7 ms. フレーム (furēmu)
Startup:
The frames from pressing a button until the attack's first active frame. Lower startup means a faster attack. 発生 (hassei)
Active Frames:
The frames during which an attack can hit. More active frames make an attack easier to land and better for anti-airs and meaty setups. 持続 (jizoku)
Recovery:
The frames after the active frames during which the character can't act and is vulnerable. 硬直 (kouchoku)
Frame Advantage:
How many frames sooner (+) or later (−) you can act than your opponent after your attack hits or is blocked. Positive means your turn; negative means theirs. 有利フレーム (yūri furēmu)
Safe / Unsafe:
A move is safe on block if the opponent can't punish it, and unsafe if they can. Exact punishability depends on how negative the move is and how fast the opponent's fastest attack is. 反撃できない (hangeki dekinai)
Hitstun:
The time a character is stuck after being hit. Hitting again before it ends creates a combo. のけぞり (nokezori)
Blockstun:
The time a character is stuck after blocking an attack, unable to do anything except keep blocking. ガード硬直 (gādo kouchoku)
Knockdown:
When a hit puts the opponent on the ground. They can't act until they get up, which sets up okizeme. ダウン (daun)
Invincibility (Invul / I-Frames):
Frames during which a move can't be hit by some or all attacks. Reversals like the shoto uppercut are typically invincible on startup. 無敵 (muteki)
Counter Hit:
Hitting the opponent during the startup (or active frames) of their attack. In SF6 it adds 2 frames of hitstun and 20% damage; in general it usually adds damage and extra hitstun, enabling bigger combos. カウンターヒット (kauntā hitto)
Punish Counter (SF6):
Hitting the opponent during the recovery of their move. In SF6 it gives 4 extra frames of hitstun, 20% extra damage and Drive Gauge drain, rewarding you for punishing. パニッシュカウンター (panisshu kauntā)
Meaty:
An attack timed so its later active frames overlap with the opponent waking up, which gives you extra frame advantage. 重ね (kasane)
Frame Trap:
A deliberate small gap in your pressure. If the opponent tries to press a button in that gap, your next attack hits them, often as a counter hit. 暴れ潰し (abare tsubushi)
Gap:
A space between two attacks in which the defender is free to act. 隙間 (sukima)
Link:
Doing a second attack after the first one fully recovers but while the opponent is still in hitstun. Links often need precise timing. 目押し (meoshi)
Cancel:
Interrupting a move's animation into another move, usually a normal into a special or super. キャンセル (kyanseru)
Chain:
Light attacks that can follow each other in quick succession, usually lights into lights. 連打キャンセル (renda kyanseru)
Target Combo:
A specific button sequence that a character can chain together as a built-in string. ターゲットコンボ (tāgetto konbo)
Buffer:
Entering part or all of an input early, during another action, so the move comes out at the first possible frame. 先行入力 (senkou nyūryoku)
Plus on Block:
An attack that leaves you with frame advantage even when blocked, letting you keep your turn. 有利 (yūri)
Shimmy:
Walking into throw range and then back out to bait a throw tech, then punishing the whiffed throw (in SF6, as a Punish Counter). シミー (shimī)
Safe Jump:
A jump attack timed to hit meaty on the opponent's wakeup while still letting you land in time to block an invincible reversal. 詐欺飛び (sagi tobi)
Back Rise / Hard Knockdown (SF6):
Back rise is SF6's wakeup option done by pressing two attack buttons as you land: you roll back a little before standing. A hard knockdown doesn't allow it (for example, a Punish Counter throw). 強制ダウン (kyousei daun)
Minus on Block:
An attack that leaves you at a frame disadvantage when blocked, so the opponent acts first. Being slightly minus (−1, −2) just gives up your turn; being very minus (−4 or worse in SF6, where the fastest normals start in 4 frames) means you can be punished if they're in range. 不利 (furi)
1-Frame Link:
A link with a single frame of timing, when the first move's advantage on hit exactly equals the second move's startup. Input buffers in modern games make it more forgiving than it sounds. 猶予1フレーム目押し (yūyo ichi furēmu meoshi)
Hitstop:
The brief freeze when an attack connects, on hit or on block. It makes hits feel heavy and gives you a little extra time to confirm and cancel. ヒットストップ (hitto sutoppu)
Pushback:
How far an attack pushes the opponent away on hit or block. Pushback decides whether your next attack reaches, and it's why long pressure strings eventually run out of range. ノックバック (nokku bakku)
Hitbox / Hurtbox:
The hitbox is the part of an attack that can hit; the hurtbox is the part of a character that can be hit. An attack connects when its hitbox touches the opponent's hurtbox, and many pokes and anti-airs win because of where these boxes sit. 攻撃判定 / 喰らい判定 (kougeki hantei / kurai hantei)
Trade:
When both players' attacks hit each other at the same time and both take damage. Some trades favor one side, like a heavy trading with a light. 相打ち (aiuchi)
Stuff:
Hitting the opponent's move during its startup so it never comes out. Stuffing an attack usually gives you a Counter Hit.
Reactable / Unreactable:
Whether an attack is slow or visible enough to answer on reaction. Most overheads and Drive Impacts are reactable; fast lows and throws generally aren't, so you have to predict them.
Delayed Tech:
Pressing throw a few frames late while blocking, so you tech a throw if it comes but still block if they strike first. It's beaten by shimmies and by attacks timed to hit the late tech. 遅らせグラップ (okurase gurappu)
Tech Trap:
An attack timed to hit an opponent who tries to tech a throw, usually a delayed tech. It's the strike version of the shimmy idea. 受身狩り, 受け身狩り (ukemi kari)
Recoverable Health:
The grey part of the health bar that refills over time if you avoid getting hit. In SF6 you take recoverable damage, for example, when your Drive Impact armor absorbs a hit or when a Drive Reversal hits you. リカバリアブルダメージ (rikabariaburu damēji)
Crumple:
A hit state where the opponent slowly collapses, giving you time for a full combo. In SF6, Drive Impact crumples when it absorbs a hit or lands as a Punish Counter, and some heavy attacks crumple on Punish Counter. 崩れ (kuzure)

Movement and Spacing

Walk Speed:
How fast a character walks. Walking keeps you able to block at any moment, which makes it the most flexible way to adjust spacing.
Dash / Backdash:
A short quick forward or backward burst of movement. Dashes cover distance fast but usually can't be canceled into blocking. Many backdashes have brief invulnerability or throw invulnerability but long recovery. ダッシュ (dasshu)
Jump-In:
An attack done from a jump onto a standing opponent, often to start a combo. Risky against a good anti-air. 飛び (tobi)
Hop / Short Hop:
A low, short jump found in some games (e.g. King of Fighters) used to approach under or over certain attacks. SF6 doesn't use hops as a general mechanic. 小ジャンプ (shou janpu)
Threat Range:
The distance at which a character's attacks can hit you. Staying just outside it is the heart of footsies. 間合い (ma ai)
Corner:
The edge of the stage. Being cornered limits your escape options and makes you easier to pressure. 画面端 (gamen hashi)
Corner Carry:
How far a combo moves the opponent toward the corner. High corner carry helps you trap them there. 画面端に運ぶ (gamen hashi ni hakobu)
Charge Input:
A special move that requires holding a direction (usually back or down) before pressing forward or up with a button, like Guile's Sonic Boom. 溜め (tame)
Air-to-Air:
Hitting an opponent in the air while you're in the air too, usually with a fast jumping normal. It's an alternative anti-air when you see the jump too late for an uppercut. 空対空 (kū tai kū)
Empty Jump:
A jump without an attack, usually to land and throw or hit low while the opponent stands up to block a jump-in that never comes. 空ジャンプ (kara janpu)
Low Profile:
Ducking under an attack with a move that lowers your hurtbox, like a slide or some crouching attacks, so higher attacks and some projectiles whiff. 低姿勢 (teishisei)
Neutral Skip:
A tool that takes you from neutral straight into offense without winning the usual footsies exchanges. In SF6 the main example is Drive Rush: a raw Drive Rush covers a lot of ground and starts your pressure.

Combos and Execution

Combo:
A sequence of attacks where each hit lands before the opponent recovers from the previous one, so they can't block or escape. A "true combo" is guaranteed once the first hit lands. コンボ (konbo)
Juggle:
A combo continued while the opponent is airborne. 空中コンボ (kūchū konbo)
BnB (Bread and Butter):
A character's standard, reliable combo that you use most often.
Hit-Confirm:
Checking whether your first hits connected before committing to the rest of a combo. On hit, continue; on block, stop with something safe. ヒット確認 (hitto kakunin)
Drop:
Failing to complete a combo you intended to do. ミス (misu)
Damage Scaling:
Each additional hit in a combo does less damage than the one before. Long combos give diminishing returns. ダメージ補正 (damēji hosei)
Reset:
Deliberately ending a combo early and putting the opponent into a mixup, betting that the mixup will lead to more damage than finishing the combo. 補正切り (hosei giri)
Block String:
A series of attacks that stays tight while the opponent blocks. Good block strings end in a move that is safe on block or leaves you plus. 固め (katame)
Tick Throw:
A light attack that gets blocked, followed by a throw. The opponent, still expecting attacks, holds block and gets thrown. 当て投げ (ate nage)
Throw Tech:
Escaping a throw by pressing throw within a short window as you are being grabbed. Prevents most of the damage. 投げ抜け (nage nuke)
Command Grab:
A special-move throw (like Zangief's Screw Piledriver). Unlike normal throws, it usually can't be teched. コマンド投げ (komando nage)
Chip Damage:
Health lost while blocking. In SF6 there is no chip damage while you have Drive Gauge: blocked specials and supers drain the gauge instead. In Burnout, specials and Super Arts chip 25% of their damage, and chip can KO. Normal attacks never chip. 削りダメージ (kezuri damēji)
DP (Dragon Punch):
The uppercut motion (623: forward, down, down-forward) and, by extension, any invincible uppercut-style reversal such as the Shoryuken. 昇竜拳 (shouryūken)
Plink:
Pressing the button you want and then a weaker one a frame later, so the game reads the first press twice and you get two chances at a tight link. Famous from SF4; not a standard technique in SF6. 辻式入力 (tsujishiki nyūryoku)
Option Select (OS):
An input that produces the right action for more than one situation, with the game choosing the outcome based on what the opponent did. Many older option selects don't work in modern games, and any of them can be beaten once the opponent recognizes it. 自動二択 (jidou nitaku)
High / Mid / Low / Overhead:
Attack heights. Mids, which include most standing attacks, can be blocked standing or crouching. Lows, like most crouching kicks and sweeps, must be blocked crouching. Overheads, like jump-ins and some command normals, must be blocked standing. Some games also have highs that crouching characters duck under. 上段 / 中段 / 下段 (joudan / chūdan / gedan)
Sweep:
A low attack, usually crouching heavy kick, that knocks the opponent down. In SF6 sweeps are very unsafe on block, so use them to whiff punish or to end combos, not as a random poke. 足払い (ashi barai)
Command Normal:
A normal done with a direction plus a button, like Ryu's Collarbone Breaker (6MP). Many overheads and special pokes are command normals. 特殊技 (tokushu waza)
Starter:
The first hit of a combo. The starter decides how much the combo can deal (SF6 scales light starters harder) and how easy it is to confirm. 始動技 (shidou waza)
Route:
The specific sequence of moves you choose for a combo from a given starter and resources. Characters often have different routes for the corner, midscreen, and with or without meter. ルート (rūto)
Conversion:
Turning a single hit into a combo or a knockdown. Good conversions mean you get the most out of every hit you land. 拾う (hirou)
Raw:
Doing something on its own instead of as part of a combo or cancel. A raw Super Art comes out from neutral; a raw Drive Rush comes from a parry instead of a canceled normal. ぶっ放し (buppanashi)
Throw Loop:
When a throw leaves you close enough, with enough advantage, to threaten another throw or a strike as they get up. Some SF6 characters have corner throw loops that force the defender to guess again and again. 投げループ (nage rūpu)
Setup:
A planned sequence after a knockdown or throw that puts the opponent in a specific situation, like a meaty, a safe jump or a crossup that's hard to read. Setups depend on the character and the knockdown. セットプレイ (setto purei)
Input Lag:
The delay between pressing a button and seeing the result on screen, added by the game, the display and the connection. More lag makes tight timing harder; a TV's game mode and a wired connection reduce it. 入力遅延 (nyūryoku chien)
Arcade Stick / Pad / Leverless:
The three main controller types. Sticks use a lever and buttons, pads are standard controllers, and leverless controllers replace the lever with a button for each direction. All three are used at the top level; pick what's comfortable. アーケードコントローラー / パッド / レバーレス (ākēdo kontorōrā / paddo / rebāresu)

Resources and SF6 Systems

Super Meter / Super Art Gauge:
The gauge used for super moves. In SF6 it fills mainly as you attack (your moves hitting or being blocked), and holds up to three bars for Level 1-3 Super Arts. SAゲージ (esu ē gēji)
Drive Gauge (SF6):
A six-bar resource shared by most SF6 defensive and offensive tools. It regenerates over time and is spent on Drive moves. Getting hit by or blocking certain attacks (like Drive Impact) drains it. ドライブゲージ (doraibu gēji)
Overdrive (OD) Moves (SF6):
Enhanced special moves that cost 2 Drive bars. The equivalent of EX moves in earlier games. オーバードライブ (ōbā doraibu)
Drive Rush (SF6):
A fast forward dash paid with Drive Gauge, either out of a Drive Parry (1 bar) or canceled from a normal (3 bars). The first normal after it gets +4 frames. Used to close distance and extend combos. ドライブラッシュ (doraibu rasshu)
Drive Parry (SF6):
A defensive stance that absorbs attacks. Successfully parrying restores some Drive Gauge, and a precisely timed "perfect parry" opens a punish window. ドライブパリィ (doraibu pari)
Drive Impact (SF6):
A slow, armored attack that absorbs hits. Blocked near the wall, it causes a wall splat that the attacker can combo from. Against a cornered opponent in Burnout, it stuns them. You can answer it with your own Drive Impact. ドライブインパクト (doraibu inpakuto)
Drive Reversal (SF6):
An invincible counterattack done from blockstun, right after a successful parry, or on wakeup, that pushes the opponent away. Costs 2 Drive bars and deals only recoverable damage. ドライブリバーサル (doraibu ribāsaru)
Burnout (SF6):
The state you enter when your Drive Gauge is empty. You can't use Drive moves, you suffer 4 extra frames of blockstun, blocked specials and Super Arts chip your health (and can KO you), and a Drive Impact that pushes you into the wall stuns you even if you block it. バーンアウト (bān auto)
EX / Enhanced Move:
A powered-up special move that costs meter, found in many fighting games. EX必殺技 (ex hissatsu waza)
Burst:
A defensive move found in some games (e.g. Guilty Gear) that breaks out of combos or pressure, usually at a resource cost. SF6 has no burst.
Armor:
A property that lets a move absorb one or more hits without being interrupted. アーマー (āmā)
Perfect Parry (SF6):
A Drive Parry that catches an attack in its first 2 frames (against strikes, you also have to hold the correct block direction). The screen freezes and you usually get a guaranteed punish, which deals only 50% damage. ジャストパリィ (jasuto pari)
Critical Art (SF6):
The stronger version of your Level 3 Super Art, available when your health is at 25% or less. It costs the same 3 bars. クリティカルアーツ (kuritikaru ātsu)
Wall Splat (SF6):
When a Drive Impact pushes the opponent into the wall, on hit or on block, they splat against it and you get a free follow-up. Against a Burnout opponent it becomes a stun. 壁貼り付け (kabe hari tsuke)
Classic / Modern / Dynamic Controls (SF6):
Classic uses six attack buttons and motion inputs; Modern uses three attack buttons plus an SP button for specials, which deal 20% less damage when done with SP. A third scheme, Dynamic, picks attacks for you and is only for offline play. クラシックタイプ / モダンタイプ / ダイナミックタイプ (kurashikku / modan / dainamikku taipu)
LP / MR (SF6):
League Points decide your rank from Rookie to Diamond; Master Rate is the separate rating used once a character reaches Master.
Install:
A move or Super Art that powers up your character for a limited time, like a mode with stronger specials. Using it well usually means activating it where the opponent can't punish the activation.
Stance:
A special state some characters enter that gives them different follow-up moves. Some stances can't block, so a stance is a risk as well as a tool. 構え (kamae)
Armor Break:
A property that beats armor: the attack hits through it instead of being absorbed. In SF6, Drive Reversal, most Super Arts and some OD moves break armor, which is one way to answer Drive Impact. アーマーブレイク (āmā bureiku)
Meterless:
Done without spending any meter. A meterless combo uses no Drive or Super Art gauge; a meterless reversal is an invincible move that costs nothing. ノーゲージ (nōgēji)
Dictator (M. Bison / Vega):
The community nickname for M. Bison. In Japan his name is Vega, and the boxer known as Balrog in the West is called M. Bison there, so players say Dictator, Boxer and Claw to avoid confusion. Keep it in mind when you read Japanese content. ベガ (bega)

Competition and Community

Round / Game / Set:
A round ends when one character runs out of health. A game (or match) is usually first to 2 rounds. A set is a series of games, usually best-of-3 or best-of-5. ラウンド (raundo)
Bracket:
A tournament structure. In double elimination, which is standard in fighting games, you're only eliminated after losing two sets.
Winners / Losers Bracket:
In double elimination, players without a set loss are in winners bracket; after one loss they drop to losers bracket. ウィナーズ側 (wināzu gawa)
Runback:
Playing the same opponent again, often right after a set. 再戦 (saisen)
Matchup:
The interaction between two specific characters. Often rated as an expected win ratio like 6-4. 組み合わせ (kumi awase)
Tier List:
A ranking of characters by competitive strength. A useful snapshot, but much less important than skill below top level. キャラランク (kyara ranku)
Meta:
The strategies and characters that are currently most effective and most common. メタ (meta)
Buff / Nerf:
A patch change that makes a character or move stronger (buff) or weaker (nerf). 強化 (kyouka)
Patch:
A game update that changes balance, fixes bugs or adds content.
Notation:
How inputs are written. Classic notation uses names like c.MK (crouching medium kick). Numpad notation uses numbers for directions, like 2MK (2 = down). 表記 (hyouki)
Tilt:
Frustration that makes you play worse. あったまってる, あったまった (attamatteru, attamatta)
FGC:
The fighting game community: the players, organizers, commentators and content creators around fighting games. 格闘ゲームコミュニティ (kakutou gēmu komyuniti)
Main / Secondary:
Your main is the character you play most and take seriously; a secondary, or pocket character, is a backup for matchups where your main struggles. メインキャラ (mein kyara)
First To (FT2, FT3):
A set format: the first player to win that many games wins the set. FT2 is the same as best of three; FT3, best of five. 2本先取 / 3本先取 (nihon senshu / sanbon senshu)
Mirror Match:
Both players using the same character. It's a pure test of fundamentals and of how well you know your own character's weaknesses. ミラーマッチ (mirā macchi)
Counter Pick:
Switching to a character that's strong against your opponent's, usually between games of a tournament set, where the loser of a game may switch and the winner keeps their character. 被せ (kabuse)
Local:
A small, recurring in-person event or meetup in your area. Locals are the best place to get real practice and honest feedback. 対戦会 (taisenkai)
Rollback Netcode:
Online code that predicts the opponent's inputs and corrects the game when the prediction is wrong, so online play feels close to offline. SF6 uses rollback; a wired connection still makes a big difference. ロールバックネットコード (rōru bakku netto kōdo)
Scrub:
In David Sirlin's sense, a player who holds themselves back with made-up rules ("throws are cheap", "fireballs are spam") instead of playing to win. More loosely, any beginner. ザコ (zako)
Casuals:
Matches played for fun or practice, with no rank or tournament at stake. Casuals are where you test new things. 野試合 (noshiai)
