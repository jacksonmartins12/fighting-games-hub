Common Mistakes & How to Fix Them

UniversalThe ideas in this chapter apply to any fighting game. Examples come from SF6, and sections marked SF6 only apply to it. For how other games differ, see Fighting Game Families.

Most players make the same errors. Here's how to identify and fix them before they become habits.

Execution Mistakes

Mistake Why It Happens How to Fix It Time to Fix
Dropping Combos in Matches Practice combos only in training mode on a dummy that stands still and gets hit every time. Real matches have: an opponent who blocks or moves, different spacing, counter-hits, nerves. Practice method: In training mode, set the dummy's guard to Random so you must hit-confirm (finish the combo on hit, stop safely on block). Record the dummy walking back and forth and confirm against that. Then play CPU or casual matches focusing only on confirms. 2-3 weeks of dedicated work
Hitting Buttons Too Slowly Fear of pressing buttons during game. Worried about losing neutral, so they hesitate on reaction. Confidence building: Record the dummy doing simple actions (walking forward, pressing a poke) and practice pressing your button on reaction. Force yourself to be aggressive 3 rounds in a row. Analyze why attacks fail (spacing, reads) not whether you should attack. 1-2 weeks
Failed Anti-Airs Either: (1) timing is off, (2) not predicting jump, (3) panicking and pressing wrong button For timing: Record the dummy jumping in at different distances (several recording slots, random playback) and anti-air every jump. For prediction: Play vs CPU and predict before they jump. For panic: Pick ONE reliable anti-air (e.g., your DP or an anti-air normal like c.HP) and use only that until it is automatic. 2-4 weeks
Shaky Inputs Weak hand/finger positioning, tension during play, using pad incorrectly Check fundamentals: Holding controller correctly? Fingers relaxed? Practice basic input (quarter-circle punch) for 5 min/day. Record yourself. Verify consistency in training mode first. 3-6 months (is ongoing)

Footsies Mistakes

Mistake Why It Happens How to Fix It Time to Fix
Standing Too Close to Opponent Impatience. Want to hit them faster. Don't understand optimal spacing for your character's longest poke. Lab work: Test your longest normal (e.g., c.MK) range. Place the dummy at that distance. Practice maintaining that exact spacing without moving forward. 20 min/session. 1 week focused
Throwing Out Unsafe Moves Don't know frame data. Throw out move without checking if it's safe. Opponent punishes every time. Frame data study: Look up your character's main pokes. Write them down (startup, advantage on hit, advantage on block, recovery). Practice using only safe moves for 1 week. Then slowly add moves that are only safe when spaced at max range. 2-3 weeks
Predictable Patterns Using same footsie sequence (e.g., c.MK, c.MK, s.HK) repeatedly. Opponent adapts and starts jumping/counter-poking. Mix-up strategy: Plan 3 different footsie sequences before match. Write them down: (1) c.MK spam, (2) c.MK→back dash, (3) s.LP→block. Deliberately rotate them in matches. 1-2 weeks
Not Building Meter While Poking Don't realize pokes affect resources. In SF6, attacks that hit or are blocked build Super Art gauge and restore Drive Gauge; just waiting refills Drive Gauge more slowly. Meter awareness: In training mode, watch both gauges while you poke. Note how much Drive Gauge comes back from landed pokes (hit or block) and from walking forward, versus doing nothing. Realize that winning footsies also wins the resource race. 3-5 days

Mental/Strategic Mistakes

Mistake Why It Happens How to Fix It Time to Fix
Tilting After Losses See loss as personal failure. Immediately queue next match in emotional state. Play worse. Tilt protocol: After 2 losses in a row, take 5-10 min break. Drink water. Do 20 push-ups. Then queue. Analyze loss objectively before next match. 1-2 weeks habits
No Adaptation Mid-Series Play same strategy every round and every game of the set. Opponent adapts. You lose without changing approach. Adaptation checklist: After each round loss, ask: (1) What did opponent do? (2) Why did it work? (3) How do I stop it next round? Answer in your head between rounds and implement it; write it down between games. 2-4 weeks
Blaming Lag/RNG Instead of Self Avoid responsibility for loss. Blame is easier than analyzing. Accountability: Watch replay. Find 3 decisions YOU made that lost the match (not opponent's good play, your choices). Write them down. Practice that scenario. Ongoing
Not Taking Notes Assume you'll remember. Don't write things down. Repeat same mistakes. Implementation: Keep phone/notebook open after sessions. Write 5 bullet points: 1 good thing, 1 thing to improve, 1 question, 1 pattern opponent used, 1 adaptation tried. Done in 5 minutes. Immediate

Matchup Knowledge Mistakes

Mistake Why It Happens How to Fix It Time to Fix
Playing Matchup Generically No character-specific knowledge. Use same strategy vs every character. Matchup deep-dive: Study 1 difficult matchup. Watch 3 pro player VODs. Take 10 notes. Lab setups specific to that character. Play 20 matches against it. 1-2 weeks per matchup
Not Knowing Opponent Frame Data Only know your own character. Don't know if opponent move is safe or punishable. Study method: Pick 3 common opponent buttons. Look them up. Is c.MK safe? What punishes them? Write down. Ask in community if unsure. 3-5 days
Underestimating Zoner Matchups Rushdown players don't respect the zoner's projectiles and anti-airs. Walk or jump forward blindly. Get controlled at the zoner's preferred range. Space management: Record the dummy throwing fireballs at different timings. Practice walking forward and blocking, jumping over fireballs on reads, and SF6 tools (Drive Parry to block them while restoring Drive Gauge, Drive Impact to armor through). Learn which ranges are safe to advance from. Play the zoner matchup 1 hour/week. 2-3 weeks
