Self-Diagnosis: Finding Your Bottleneck

UniversalThe ideas in this chapter apply to any fighting game. Examples come from SF6, and sections marked SF6 only apply to it. For how other games differ, see Fighting Game Families.

You're stuck. You know it. But where are you stuck? This diagnostic flowchart helps you identify your specific weakness so you can fix it faster.

Start Here: What Describes Your Situation?

Answer These Questions Honestly

Question 1: When You Lose, What Happens Most?

  • A) You get hit by the same move repeatedly. They figure you out and you can't adapt.
  • B) You mess up combos. The execution is there in training, but not in matches.
  • C) You get zoned out or keep walking into traps. Neutral game is bad.
  • D) You tilt and play worse. Mental game is the issue.

Diagnostic Tree

Path A: "I Get Hit by Same Move, Can't Adapt"

Root Issue: Weak pattern recognition OR low adaptation speed.

Sub-Questions:

  • Can you describe what they're doing? (E.g., "They jump every time I poke")
    • YES → You see the pattern. Issue is execution of counter. Go to: Practice Anti-Air/Counter Response (20 min/day, 2 weeks)
    • NO → You don't see the pattern. Issue is observation. Go to: Watch replays and pause between rounds. Ask: "What did they just do 3 times in a row?"

Primary Fix (2-3 weeks): After EVERY loss, identify ONE thing opponent repeated. Write it down. Lab counter for 30 min.

Path B: "Execution Fails in Matches but Works in Training"

Root Issue: Execution under pressure, not raw technical skill.

Sub-Questions:

  • Which part fails most? (Combo startup, mid-combo confirm, ender?)
    • Startup → Problem: Not confirming in time, or the starter link/cancel timing is off. Fix: Practice the confirm 100x against a dummy set to Random guard that is recorded moving.
    • Mid-combo → Problem: Link or cancel timing (pressing too early or too late), usually worse under nerves. Fix: Turn on input history and the frame meter to see whether you are early or late. If needed, lower the game speed in training settings to learn the rhythm, then return to normal speed.
    • Ender → Problem: Panic before finisher. Fix: Practice ender in isolation 50x until it's unconscious.

Primary Fix (2-4 weeks): Practice confirms against a Random-guard dummy and in CPU matches (Level 2-3) every day. Record yourself. Verify consistency improving week-to-week.

Path C: "Neutral Game / Footsies Weak"

Root Issue: Range control, poke selection, or spacing awareness.

Sub-Questions:

  • Do you know your character's best poke? (c.MK, c.LK, s.LP?)
    • NO → Go read frame data. Identify longest normal. Practice maintaining that range. 30 min/day, 1 week.
    • YES → Do you know when it's safe? Can you hit it at max range without retaliation?
      • NO → Lab it. Know startup, advantage on block (check it on the frame meter), recovery. Practice spacing at max range only.
      • YES → Issue is poke selection variety. You're too predictable. Practice alternating 2-3 different footsie sequences per match.

Primary Fix (1-4 weeks depending): Identify weakness (knowledge vs execution), address it, then record 10 matches and analyze whether footsies improved.

Path D: "Mental Game / Tilting"

Root Issue: Emotional control or confidence.

Sub-Questions:

  • When do you tilt?
    • After 1-2 losses → Low tolerance for failure. Fix: Play sessions judged by one skill goal (e.g., "anti-air every jump"), not by wins. Expect losing streaks. Normalize losing.
    • When behind in match → Low confidence. Fix: Practice comebacks. Review replays of rounds where you were far behind on health and still won, and note what turned them around.
    • When opponent reads you → Ego issue. Fix: Reframe as "they're adapting, now I adapt back" not "I'm bad".
    • During specific matchup → Matchup-specific fear. Fix: Play that matchup 50 times. Realize you're not helpless.

Primary Fix (Ongoing): Implement 5-min break protocol after 2 losses. Journal after sessions (5 min). Track emotional state week-to-week. Normalize failing as part of learning.
