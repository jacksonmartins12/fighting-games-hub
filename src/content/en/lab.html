Lab Exercises: Structured Practice Sessions

UniversalThe ideas in this chapter apply to any fighting game. Examples come from SF6, and sections marked SF6 only apply to it. For how other games differ, see Fighting Game Families.

Training mode can feel aimless. These lab exercises are structured 20-30 minute sessions designed to level up specific skills. Pick the one matching your current weakness and do it daily for 1-2 weeks.

Lab Exercise 1: Anti-Air Precision (20 min)

Goal: 80% anti-air success rate against common jump-in patterns

Breakdown

  1. (2 min) Warmup: Do your anti-air move (DP, an anti-air normal like c.HP, etc.) 20 times. Get feel for the input.
  2. (5 min) Spot practice: Record the dummy doing a forward jump-in from a fixed distance and play it back. Practice anti-air 30 times. Record success rate.
  3. (5 min) Distance practice: Record jump-ins from closer and farther distances (including a deep jump-in that lands right in front of you). Practice 30 times.
  4. (5 min) Crossup practice: Record a jump-in that crosses over your head and lands behind you. Practice 30 times (remember that when they cross over, your input direction switches sides, and the anti-air may need to be timed later).
  5. (2 min) Mixed patterns: Put the different jumps in separate recording slots and set playback to random. Practice 15 times. See consistency.
  6. (1 min) Record your results. Did you hit 80%+ success on each pattern?

Lab Exercise 2: Footsies Range Control (25 min)

Goal: Master one poke, land it from optimal spacing at least 95 times out of 100 without whiffing

Breakdown

  1. (2 min) Frame data review: Look up your best poke (c.MK likely). Verify range, startup, advantage on block, recovery.
  2. (3 min) Distance measurement: Set the dummy to stand still. Find exactly the range where your poke reaches but the opponent's common poke does not (test theirs by recording the dummy pressing it). This is your optimal range.
  3. (15 min) Execution: Set the dummy to guard all. Perform poke 100 times from optimal range. Count successes. Goal: 95+ pokes that connect (hit or block) instead of whiffing.
  4. (3 min) Against movement: Record the dummy walking forward and back at random. Still maintain range and poke 30 times as they step into it.
  5. (2 min) Analysis: Did you maintain spacing? Any whiffs? Record notes.

Lab Exercise 3: Combo Confirm Under Pressure (25 min)

Goal: Make the correct confirm decision 25+ times out of 35 against a dummy that randomly blocks, moves and mashes

Breakdown

  1. (3 min) Clean practice: Dummy stands still with no guard. Land BnB combo 5 times perfectly. Get muscle memory.
  2. (5 min) Confirm only: Set the dummy's guard to Random. Hit with your combo starter (e.g., c.MK). Finish the combo only when it hits; stop at a safe point when it is blocked. 15 attempts.
  3. (7 min) Moving dummy: Random guard plus a recording of the dummy walking forward and back. 10 attempts. Is movement breaking your spacing or your confirms?
  4. (7 min) Mashing dummy: Random guard plus a reversal setting that makes the dummy press a fast button after blocking. 10 attempts. Any gap in your string gets interrupted, and a combo you finish on block gets punished. This is hardest. Precision over speed.
  5. (3 min) Record results. How many of the 35 attempts were correct? Where did you drop combos or confirm wrong?

Lab Exercise 4: Adaptation Scenario (30 min)

Goal: Test three different strategies in separate blocks and identify the one that works best

Breakdown

  1. (2 min) Preparation: Write down 3 different strategies. Example: (1) Poke heavy, (2) Aggressive pressure, (3) Defensive counter-punish.
  2. (8 min) Block 1: Play CPU (or, better, a human in casual matches, since a CPU does not adapt like a person) using strategy #1. Does it work? Why/why not?
  3. (8 min) Block 2: Play using strategy #2 (different). Did changing approach help?
  4. (8 min) Block 3: Play using strategy #3. Then compare all three: which one actually won?
  5. (4 min) Reflection: Which strategy worked? Could you identify opponent's weakness and exploit it?

Lab Exercise 5: Meter Management Awareness (20 min)SF6

Goal: Win a match spending meter only on purpose (no wasted Drive Gauge, no careless Burnout)

Breakdown

  1. (3 min) Theory: Review SF6's two resources. Drive Gauge (6 bars): Drive Impact 1 bar, Drive Rush from Drive Parry 1 bar, Drive Rush as a cancel 3 bars, OD moves 2 bars, Drive Reversal 2 bars; empty it and you are in Burnout. Super Art gauge (3 bars): Level 1, 2 or 3 Super Arts cost 1, 2 or 3 bars (at low health, the Level 3 becomes a Critical Art). When is each worth spending?
  2. (12 min) Practice match: Play CPU (Level 3). After every round, audit both gauges. Did you build them? Spend them wisely? Did you go into Burnout?
  3. (3 min) Replay watch: Watch match back. Identify 2 meter spends that were good, 1 that was wasteful.
  4. (2 min) Repeat: Play another match. Apply lesson from replay.

Lab Exercise 6: Learning a New Character (40 min)

Goal: Understand what a character does by testing it yourself, before relying on guides

Breakdown

  1. (5 min) Unique mechanic: find what makes the character different (a stance, a stock, an install, a special Super Art) and what it rewards. Plan the rest of the checklist around it.
  2. (10 min) Normals: for each one, note its maximum range, whether it's cancelable, whether it works as an anti-air, what it's for and its basic frame data (startup, on block).
  3. (5 min) Punish Counter effects: find which heavy normals crumple, spin or launch on Punish Counter.
  4. (2 min) Throw range: walk back from the dummy until the throw whiffs.
  5. (10 min) Specials, the same way as the normals, then their OD versions: what does OD add (invincibility, extra hits, better frame data)?
  6. (5 min) Super Arts: which are invincible, which combo from what, which make good punishes.
  7. (3 min) Write three lines: how this character wins, what they fear, and one thing to punish. Then compare with the character's page in this guide.

Lab Exercise 7: Build and Test a Pressure String (25 min)

Goal: Two pressure strings for your character that you've verified yourself, one safe and one frame trap

Breakdown

  1. (3 min) Open your character's frame table: sort your normals by block advantage, then by startup.
  2. (5 min) Using the gap rule from Advanced Mechanics, write two candidates: a true block string that ends safe, and a frame trap with a confirmable button in the gap.
  3. (5 min) Set the dummy to crouch and block everything. Check that every hit reaches and how many hits fit before pushback.
  4. (5 min) Record the dummy doing your string and try every normal you have against it. Anything that gets out marks a gap you didn't intend.
  5. (5 min) Set the dummy to press a fast button after blocking. Your frame trap should Counter Hit it every time; practice confirming into your combo.
  6. (2 min) Repeat the test in the corner and note which string is safe where.

Lab Exercise 8: Emulate and Compare (30 min)

Goal: Find five decisions where a top player chose differently than you would, understand why, and try one of them yourself

Breakdown

  1. (3 min) Pick a high-level replay or tournament match of your character against a character you struggle with.
  2. (15 min) Pause at each knockdown, each corner situation and each neutral exchange that looks important. Before you unpause, write down what you would do and what you expect the opponent to do. Then watch what happened.
  3. (5 min) Go through the differences. For each one, write why the player's choice might be better: resources, position, what the opponent had been doing.
  4. (5 min) Pick one of those choices, recreate the situation in training mode (position, Drive Gauge, meter) and try their option until it feels natural.
  5. (2 min) Note it in your practice journal and try it in your next casual matches.

Lab Exercise 9: Restricted Rounds (20 min)

Goal: Learn to control space on the ground by taking away the shortcuts, as the Footsies Handbook recommends

Breakdown

  1. (5 min) Play full rounds without jumping. Every approach has to be walked, so you learn which normals hold your ground.
  2. (5 min) Play full rounds without dashing or Drive Rush. Move only by walking and notice how much more of the opponent's game you see.
  3. (5 min) Play rounds where you may guess with an invincible reversal only once. After that one, you have to block or anti-air on reaction.
  4. (5 min) Play with all three rules at once, then write down which rule was hardest to keep. That's the habit you lean on most.
